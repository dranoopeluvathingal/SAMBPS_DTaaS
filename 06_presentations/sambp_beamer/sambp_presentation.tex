    IEEE~C37.300-2024 mandates adaptive CPC supervisory layer.

  %% ---- SPEAKER NOTES ------------------------------------------------
  \note{
    \textbf{[Slide 2 --- Background and Context]}\\[4pt]
    The left chart shows the generation mix transformation.
    By 2030, IBRs are projected to supply 72\% of generation
    while synchronous machines drop to 28\%.
    This is already happening in India's renewable-rich states.\\[4pt]
    The right chart shows the \textbf{critical technical consequence}.
    A synchronous generator produces 8--10\,pu fault current.
    An inverter-based resource is software-limited to 1.1--2.0\,pu.\\[4pt]
    The red dotted line is a typical overcurrent relay pickup set for
    synchronous machines.
    When an IBR feeds a fault, the current simply does not reach
    the pickup threshold --- the relay does not see a fault at all.\\[4pt]
    This is the physical root of every failure mode I will show next.
    IEEE C37.300-2024, the new CPC systems standard, explicitly
    mandates an adaptive supervisory layer for exactly this reason.
  }
\end{frame}

      \vspace{0.2cm}
      \begin{alertblock}{Core Finding}
        \scriptsize
        Through-fault operate ratio:
        $\rho = \dfrac{\Idiff}{\Ibias} = \dfrac{2(1-\kibr)}{1+\kibr}$\\[4pt]
        Fixed $k_m$ fails at \emph{both} extremes of $\kibr$
      \end{alertblock}
  \end{columns}

  %% ---- SPEAKER NOTES ------------------------------------------------
  \note{
    \textbf{[Appendix C --- Protection Challenges, Ch.\ 3]}\\[4pt]
    The left figure shows the 87L failure plane:
    the $I_{diff}$--$I_{bias}$ plane with the fixed slope.
    Red crosses are missed trips (IBR below slope line);
    orange squares are false trip risks (IBR drives $I_{diff}$ above slope
    during through-fault conditions).\\[4pt]
    The sympathetic tripping diagram on the right shows why through-fault
    $I_{diff}$ is non-zero when IBR inject into a fault on a parallel feeder.\\[4pt]
    The core finding is the operate ratio formula:
    $\rho = 2(1-k_{ibr}) / (1+k_{ibr})$.
    When $k_{ibr} \to 1$, $\rho \to 0$ (false trip risk);
    when $k_{ibr} \to 0$, $\rho \to 2$ (missed trip if slope $> 2$).
    A fixed slope cannot satisfy both simultaneously.
  }
\end{frame}
