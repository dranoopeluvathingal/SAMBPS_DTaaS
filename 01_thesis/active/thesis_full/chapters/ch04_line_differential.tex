\chapter{IBR-Adaptive Line Current Differential Protection}
\label{ch:line_diff}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:ch4_intro}

Line current differential (87L) protection is the most selective and most
reliable protection scheme available for transmission and sub-transmission
lines: it requires no voltage measurement, is immune to power swings, and
can detect high-impedance faults that defeat distance and overcurrent relays.
However, as established in Section~\ref{sec:ch3_87l}, the fixed-slope bias
characteristic of the classical 87L relay becomes unreliable when the IBR
current ratio $\kibr$ departs from the narrow range for which the slope
was calibrated.

This chapter develops four complementary adaptive extensions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1}: Physics-constrained adaptive threshold (Section~\ref{sec:ch4_adaptive}).
\item \textbf{C2}: Harmonic-discrimination 87LH element (Section~\ref{sec:ch4_harmonic}).
\item \textbf{C3}: Negative-sequence 87LN element (Section~\ref{sec:ch4_87ln}).
\item \textbf{C4}: Transient differential current signature TDCS-87L
      (Section~\ref{sec:ch4_tdcs}).
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:ch4_coordination} describes the hierarchical vote logic
that combines the four elements. Section~\ref{sec:ch4_stability} provides
stability proofs. Section~\ref{sec:ch4_results} presents performance results.

\subsection{Literature Context}

The foundational 87L differential relay principle was established by
\citet{Westinghouse1962}. Modern communication-assisted 87L relays
(SEL-311L, GE L90) use percentage-bias characteristics defined in
IEC~60255-87 \citep{IEC60255}. The impact of IBR limited fault current on
87L performance was first noted by \citet{Hooshyar2019}; however, their
analysis was restricted to a two-terminal radial system with a single GFL
at rated output. The present work extends the analysis to:
(i) variable $\kibr \in [0.03, 0.85]$,
(ii) multi-terminal lines with mixed GFL/GFM sources,
(iii) high-impedance faults with $R_F$ up to 100~$\Omega$, and
(iv) an online adaptive scheme that does not require pre-computed setting
tables.

\section{Classical 87L Review}
\label{sec:ch4_review}

The classical percentage-bias differential relay defines:
\begin{align}
  I_\mathrm{diff} &= |\phasor{I}_A + \phasor{I}_B|
  \label{eq:ch4_idiff_def} \\
  I_\mathrm{bias} &= \frac{|\phasor{I}_A| + |\phasor{I}_B|}{2}
  \label{eq:ch4_ibias_def}
\end{align}
The relay operates when the operating criterion is satisfied:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{I_\mathrm{diff} > k_m \cdot I_\mathrm{bias} + I_0}
  \label{eq:ch4_87l_criterion}
\end{equation}
with fixed slope $k_m \in [0.20, 0.40]$ and minimum pickup $I_0$.

\subsection{Through-Fault (Restrain) Analysis}

For a healthy line carrying through-load current $I_L$:
$\phasor{I}_A \approx I_L\angle 0°$ and $\phasor{I}_B \approx -I_L\angle 0°$
(equal and opposite). In the ideal case:
$I_\mathrm{diff} = 0$, $I_\mathrm{bias} = I_L$.
With CT ratio error $\epsilon_\mathrm{CT} \leq 1\%$ (Class 1P):
\begin{equation}
  I_\mathrm{diff}^\mathrm{CT} \leq 2\epsilon_\mathrm{CT} I_\mathrm{bias}
  \label{eq:ch4_ct_error}
\end{equation}
The stability margin (distance from CT error to operate boundary) is:
\begin{equation}
  \eta = k_m - 2\epsilon_\mathrm{CT} = k_m - 0.02
  \label{eq:ch4_stability_margin}
\end{equation}
For $k_m = 0.25$, $\eta = 0.23$: the relay is stable for CT errors up to
12.5\% before false tripping. Setting $k_m$ too low reduces $\eta$.

\subsection{Internal Fault (Operate) Analysis}

For an internal bolted fault at location $\alpha$:
\begin{align}
  \phasor{I}_A &= \frac{\phasor{V}_0}{Z_S + \alpha Z_L}
  \label{eq:ch4_ia_fault} \\
  \phasor{I}_B &= \kibr \cdot I_\mathrm{rated} \angle \phi_B
  \label{eq:ch4_ib_ibr}
\end{align}
The differential current is:
\begin{equation}
  I_\mathrm{diff} = |\phasor{I}_A + \phasor{I}_B|
    = \sqrt{I_A^2 + I_B^2 + 2I_A I_B \cos(\phi_A - \phi_B)}
  \label{eq:ch4_idiff_fault}
\end{equation}
The restraint current is $I_\mathrm{bias} = (I_A + I_B)/2$.
The operate-to-bias ratio for the fixed-slope relay is:
\begin{equation}
  \rho = \frac{I_\mathrm{diff}}{I_\mathrm{bias}} =
  \frac{2\sqrt{I_A^2 + I_B^2 + 2I_A I_B \cos\Delta\phi}}{I_A + I_B}
  \label{eq:ch4_rho}
\end{equation}
Operation requires $\rho > k_m$ (neglecting $I_0$). The minimum $\rho$
occurs when $\cos\Delta\phi = -1$ (sources fully in-phase opposition):
\begin{equation}
  \rho_\mathrm{min} = \frac{2|I_A - I_B|}{I_A + I_B}
  = \frac{2(1 - \kibr)}{1 + \kibr} \quad (\text{for }I_A = 1, I_B = \kibr)
  \label{eq:ch4_rho_min}
\end{equation}
For $\kibr = 0.1$: $\rho_\mathrm{min} = 1.636$ (operate, easy).
For $\kibr = 0.4$: $\rho_\mathrm{min} = 0.857$ (operate if $k_m < 0.86$).
For $\kibr = 0.5$: $\rho_\mathrm{min} = 0.667$ (fail if $k_m > 0.67$).

This analysis shows that as $\kibr$ increases toward 0.5 (equal currents
from both ends), the operate-to-bias ratio falls toward 0 for through-fault
conditions, making discrimination impossible with a fixed slope.

\section{Physics-Constrained Adaptive 87L Threshold}
\label{sec:ch4_adaptive}

\subsection{Adaptive Slope Derivation}

The key insight is that the optimal slope is not fixed but is a function
of the instantaneous IBR current ratio $\kibr$:

\begin{thesisdef}{Optimal Adaptive Bias Slope}
The optimal bias slope $k_m^\ast(\kibr)$ is the minimum slope that
simultaneously satisfies:
(a) stability (no false trip during CT errors up to $\epsilon_\mathrm{CT}$), and
(b) sensitivity (guaranteed trip for internal faults with resistance up
to $R_F^\mathrm{max}$):
\begin{equation}
  k_m^\ast(\kibr) = \max\!\left[k_m^\mathrm{stab}(\kibr),\;\;
                              \frac{k_m^\mathrm{sens}(\kibr)}{1 - \delta_\mathrm{margin}}\right]
  \label{eq:ch4_km_star}
\end{equation}
\end{thesisdef}

\subsubsection{Stability Constraint}

For through-fault stability with CT error $\epsilon$ and
charging current $I_c$:
\begin{equation}
  k_m^\mathrm{stab}(\kibr) = 2\epsilon_\mathrm{CT}
    + \frac{I_c}{I_\mathrm{bias}} + \frac{I_\mathrm{CVT\,transient}}{I_\mathrm{bias}}
    \geq 2\epsilon_\mathrm{CT} + \frac{\omega C Z_L I_\mathrm{bias}}{2I_\mathrm{bias}}
  \label{eq:ch4_km_stab}
\end{equation}
where $C$ is the line charging capacitance. For a 33~kV cable with
$C = 0.3\;\mu\mathrm{F/km}$ over 10~km:
$k_m^\mathrm{stab} \approx 0.02 + 0.006\kibr + 0.03 = 0.05 + 0.006\kibr$.

\subsubsection{Sensitivity Constraint}

For an internal fault at location $\alpha$ with fault resistance $R_F$:
\begin{align}
  \phasor{I}_\mathrm{diff} &= \phasor{I}_A + \phasor{I}_B
  = \phasor{I}_A\left(1 + \frac{R_F}{Z_S + \alpha Z_L}\right)^{-1}
    + \kibr I_r e^{\jj\phi_B}
  \label{eq:ch4_idiff_rf} \\
  k_m^\mathrm{sens}(\kibr) &=
  \frac{|\phasor{I}_\mathrm{diff}|_{\min}}{I_\mathrm{bias}}
  = f(\alpha, R_F, \kibr, Z_S, Z_L)
  \label{eq:ch4_km_sens}
\end{align}
Evaluating at the worst-case operating point $(\alpha = 1.0, R_F = R_F^\mathrm{max})$
and $\kibr$ from real-time measurement:
\begin{equation}
  k_m^\mathrm{sens}(\kibr) = \frac{2\sqrt{1 + \kibr^2
    - 2\kibr\cos(\phi_A - \phi_B + \psi)}}{1 + \kibr}
  \label{eq:ch4_km_sens2}
\end{equation}
where $\psi = \arg(Z_F/(Z_S + Z_L)) + \pi$.

\subsubsection{Closed-Form Adaptive Slope}

Combining the stability and sensitivity constraints and choosing
$\delta_\mathrm{margin} = 0.05$ (5\% design margin):
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{k_m^\ast(\kibr) = \frac{0.5(1 - \kibr)^2}{(1 + \kibr)^2}
    + \frac{0.02\kibr}{1 - \kibr + 10^{-3}} + 0.05}
  \label{eq:ch4_km_formula}
\end{equation}

This piecewise expression captures:
\begin{itemize}
\item For low $\kibr$ (weak IBR): $k_m^\ast \approx 0.5$, providing
      a wide stability margin.
\item For moderate $\kibr$ (nominal): $k_m^\ast \approx 0.2$--$0.3$,
      matching the classical setting.
\item For high $\kibr$ (strong IBR): $k_m^\ast$ decreases toward
      $0.05 + \epsilon$, maximising sensitivity.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:ch4_adaptive_slope} shows $k_m^\ast(\kibr)$ compared to
the classical fixed slope.

\subsection{Real-Time \texorpdfstring{$\kibr$}{k\_ibr} Estimation}

The relay estimates $\kibr$ from its local current measurement using:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\kibr}(t) = \frac{|\phasor{I}_B(t)|}{I_\mathrm{rated}}
  \label{eq:ch4_kibr_est}
\end{equation}
where $\phasor{I}_B$ is the received current phasor from the remote relay
(available over IEC~61850 GOOSE within 4~ms). A first-order exponential
filter smooths measurement noise:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\kibr}(t) \leftarrow (1-\lambda)\hat{\kibr}(t-1) + \lambda\hat{\kibr}_\mathrm{raw}(t)
  \label{eq:ch4_kibr_filter}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda = 0.1$ (time constant $\approx 10$ samples at 1~kHz).

\subsection{Complete Adaptive 87L Algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:ch4_adaptive87l} states the complete relay logic.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Adaptive 87L Threshold (C1)}
\label{alg:ch4_adaptive87l}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Current phasors $\phasor{I}_A$, $\phasor{I}_B$ at 1~kHz sample rate
\Require Rated current $I_\mathrm{rated}$, CT error bound $\epsilon_\mathrm{CT}$
\Ensure Trip signal $T \in \{0, 1\}$
\State $\hat{\kibr} \leftarrow |\phasor{I}_B| / I_\mathrm{rated}$ \Comment{Estimate IBR ratio}
\State Apply exponential filter (Eq.~\ref{eq:ch4_kibr_filter})
\State $k_m \leftarrow k_m^\ast(\hat{\kibr})$ via Eq.~(\ref{eq:ch4_km_formula})
\State $I_\mathrm{diff} \leftarrow |\phasor{I}_A + \phasor{I}_B|$
\State $I_\mathrm{bias} \leftarrow (|\phasor{I}_A| + |\phasor{I}_B|)/2$
\If{$I_\mathrm{diff} > k_m \cdot I_\mathrm{bias} + I_0$}
  \State $T_1 \leftarrow 1$ \Comment{87L criterion met}
\Else
  \State $T_1 \leftarrow 0$
\EndIf
\State \Return $T_1$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Harmonic-Discrimination 87LH Element}
\label{sec:ch4_harmonic}

IBR fault currents contain significant harmonic content during the first
1--2 cycles post-fault due to filter resonance and current controller
dynamics. This signature distinguishes IBR faults from transformer inrush
(which also produces 2nd harmonic, but with no fundamental current
differential). The 87LH element uses the 2nd and 5th harmonic components
of the differential current.

\subsection{IBR Harmonic Signature Model}

The IBR output current during fault inception is:
\begin{equation}
  i_\mathrm{IBR}(t) = I_1\cos(\omega_0 t + \phi_1)
    + I_2\cos(2\omega_0 t + \phi_2)
    + I_5\cos(5\omega_0 t + \phi_5) + \cdots
  \label{eq:ch4_ibr_harmonic}
\end{equation}
The 2nd harmonic ratio for a GFL inverter with 500~Hz bandwidth current
loop is:
\begin{equation}
  h_2 = \frac{I_2}{I_1} = \frac{\omega_0}{2\omega_0}
    \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + (2\omega_0/\omega_\mathrm{bw})^2}}
  \approx 0.15 \quad \text{(typical)}
  \label{eq:ch4_h2_ratio}
\end{equation}
For a transformer inrush, $h_2 > 0.20$ and the fundamental differential
current is near zero. The discrimination criterion is:

\begin{equation}
  \text{IBR fault}: \quad I_1^\mathrm{diff} > I_0
  \quad\text{AND}\quad h_2 < 0.20
  \label{eq:ch4_87lh_ibr}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \text{Inrush}: \quad h_2 > 0.20
  \quad\text{OR}\quad I_1^\mathrm{diff} < I_0
  \label{eq:ch4_87lh_inrush}
\end{equation}

\subsection{87LH Operating Criterion}

The 87LH element operates when:
\begin{equation}
  I_\mathrm{diff,1} > k_m \cdot I_\mathrm{bias,1} + I_0
  \quad\text{AND}\quad \frac{I_\mathrm{diff,2}}{I_\mathrm{diff,1}} < h_\mathrm{block} = 0.15
  \label{eq:ch4_87lh_criterion}
\end{equation}
where the subscripts 1 and 2 denote fundamental and second-harmonic
components respectively. The harmonic ratio threshold $h_\mathrm{block} = 0.15$
is lower than the standard inrush blocking threshold of 0.20, providing
a 5\% margin.

\subsection{Multi-Inverter Harmonic Cancellation}

When multiple IBRs are connected at one line end, their harmonic currents
may cancel due to phase dispersion (each IBR's PLL locks to a slightly
different phase). The worst-case cancellation is:
\begin{equation}
  I_\mathrm{diff,2}^\mathrm{total} \geq I_\mathrm{diff,2}^\mathrm{single} \cdot \cos(N\Delta\phi/2)
  \label{eq:ch4_harmonic_cancel}
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the number of IBRs and $\Delta\phi$ is the inter-IBR phase
dispersion. For $N \leq 5$ and $\Delta\phi \leq 5°$: cancellation < 10\%,
negligible impact. For $N > 10$: the harmonic discrimination element
should be disabled and the threshold $h_\mathrm{block}$ set to zero.

\section{Negative-Sequence 87LN Element}
\label{sec:ch4_87ln}

For asymmetric faults (SLG, LL, LLG) that produce negative-sequence
current, the 87LN element provides a parallel operate path that is
insensitive to $\kibr$ (because the IBR's negative-sequence suppression
reduces $I_\mathrm{bias,2}$, making the negative-sequence differential
more sensitive, not less).

\subsection{Negative-Sequence Differential Current}

The negative-sequence differential current under an SLG fault is:
\begin{align}
  \phasor{I}_\mathrm{diff,2} &= \phasor{I}_{A,2} + \phasor{I}_{B,2}
  \label{eq:ch4_idiff2}
\end{align}
With IBR negative-sequence suppression ($\phasor{I}_{B,2}^\mathrm{IBR} = 0$):
\begin{equation}
  \phasor{I}_\mathrm{diff,2} = \phasor{I}_{A,2}
  = \frac{\phasor{V}_2^\mathrm{flt}}{Z_{2,S} + \alpha Z_L}
  \label{eq:ch4_idiff2_ibr}
\end{equation}
The negative-sequence restraint current is:
$I_\mathrm{bias,2} = (I_{A,2} + I_{B,2})/2 = I_{A,2}/2$
(since $I_{B,2} = 0$). Therefore:
\begin{equation}
  \rho_2 = \frac{I_\mathrm{diff,2}}{I_\mathrm{bias,2}} = 2
  \label{eq:ch4_rho2}
\end{equation}
Regardless of $k_m$, $\rho_2 = 2$ always exceeds the slope threshold,
guaranteeing operation for any asymmetric fault with IBR negative-sequence
suppression. This is an important result: IBR suppression of negative-sequence
current \emph{improves} 87LN sensitivity, inverting the conventional wisdom
that IBR sequence suppression is always detrimental.

\begin{proposition}[87LN Sensitivity with IBR Negative-Sequence Suppression]
\label{prop:ch4_87ln}
If the IBR at line end~B suppresses negative-sequence current
($I_{B,2} = 0$), then the negative-sequence differential-to-bias ratio
$\rho_2 = 2$ irrespective of $\kibr$, and the 87LN element operates for
all asymmetric internal faults with $I_{A,2} > I_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:ch4_idiff2_ibr}: $I_\mathrm{diff,2} = I_{A,2}$.
From the bias definition: $I_\mathrm{bias,2} = (I_{A,2} + 0)/2 = I_{A,2}/2$.
Therefore $\rho_2 = I_{A,2}/(I_{A,2}/2) = 2$. \qquad $\square$
\end{proof}

\subsection{87LN Operating Criterion}

The 87LN element operates when:
\begin{equation}
  I_\mathrm{diff,2} > k_{m,2} \cdot I_\mathrm{bias,2} + I_{0,2}
  \label{eq:ch4_87ln_criterion}
\end{equation}
with $k_{m,2} = 1.5$ (below the $\rho_2 = 2$ guarantee) and $I_{0,2} = 0.05$~pu.

\subsection{87LN0 Extension for Zero-Sequence}

When a grounding transformer is present at the IBR end, zero-sequence
current $I_0$ is injected. A parallel 87LN0 element monitors the
zero-sequence differential:
\begin{equation}
  I_\mathrm{diff,0} = |I_{A,0} + I_{B,0}|
    > k_{m,0} \cdot I_\mathrm{bias,0} + I_{0,\mathrm{min}}
  \label{eq:ch4_87ln0_criterion}
\end{equation}
with $k_{m,0} = 0.5$ and $I_{0,\mathrm{min}} = 0.02$~pu.

\section{Transient Differential Current Signature (TDCS-87L)}
\label{sec:ch4_tdcs}

Three-phase faults involving purely IBR-fed lines produce zero
negative- and zero-sequence current at the relay terminal
($I_2 = I_0 = 0$). The 87LH and 87LN elements are therefore inactive.
For these faults, the TDCS-87L element exploits the transient current
signature in the first 5~ms post-fault.

\subsection{Physical Basis}

When a fault occurs at location $\alpha$ at time $t_0$, the travelling
wave launched from the fault point arrives at relay end~A at:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_A = \alpha \ell / v_p
  \label{eq:ch4_tdcs_tau}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the line length and $v_p \approx 0.8c$ is the wave
propagation speed. The transient current increment at time $t_0 + \tau_A$
is:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta I_A(t_0 + \tau_A) = -\frac{V_\mathrm{pre}}{Z_c}
  \label{eq:ch4_tdcs_di}
\end{equation}
where $Z_c = \sqrt{L/C}$ is the surge impedance. This transient is
detectable in the 2--5~kHz frequency range. The TDCS score is:
\begin{equation}
  S_\mathrm{TDCS}(t) = \sum_{k=t-N_w}^{t}
  \left[\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(I_\mathrm{diff}(t)\bigr)\right]^2 \Delta t
  \label{eq:ch4_tdcs_score}
\end{equation}
where $N_w$ is a 5~ms sliding window. An internal fault produces a sharp
spike in $S_\mathrm{TDCS}$ within the first 5~ms.

\subsection{TDCS Operating Criterion}

The TDCS-87L element operates when:
\begin{equation}
  S_\mathrm{TDCS} > S_\mathrm{threshold}
  \quad\text{AND}\quad
  I_\mathrm{diff} > I_0
  \label{eq:ch4_tdcs_criterion}
\end{equation}
The threshold $S_\mathrm{threshold}$ is set at 3$\sigma$ above the RMS
TDCS score under through-load conditions, where $\sigma$ is measured from
a 100-cycle pre-fault training window.

\section{Hierarchical Voting Logic}
\label{sec:ch4_coordination}

The four elements (adaptive 87L, 87LH, 87LN, TDCS-87L) form a
hierarchical vote:

\begin{equation}
  T_\mathrm{87L} = T_1 \cup T_2 \cup T_3 \cup T_4
  \label{eq:ch4_vote}
\end{equation}
where $T_i$ denotes the trip signal from element $i$. The trip is issued
if \emph{any} element operates. The elements are designed with non-overlapping
fault type coverage:
\begin{itemize}
\item $T_1$ (adaptive 87L): all fault types, normal operating range.
\item $T_2$ (87LH): IBR-induced harmonic faults; blocks transformer inrush.
\item $T_3$ (87LN): asymmetric faults with IBR negative-sequence suppression.
\item $T_4$ (TDCS): three-phase faults on IBR-only lines.
\end{itemize}

A blocking signal from the TDCS element inhibits the adaptive 87L when
the capacitive charging current is too high, preventing false trips during
energisation:
\begin{equation}
  B_\mathrm{87L} = \mathbf{1}\!\left[\frac{I_c}{I_\mathrm{bias}} > 0.10\right]
  \label{eq:ch4_block}
\end{equation}

\section{Stability and Security Analysis}
\label{sec:ch4_stability}

\begin{proposition}[Stability under CT Saturation]
\label{prop:ch4_stability}
The adaptive 87L relay with slope $k_m^\ast(\kibr)$ as defined in
\eqref{eq:ch4_km_formula} does not operate for any through-load condition
provided that the CT composite error satisfies:
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_\mathrm{CT} \leq \frac{k_m^\ast(\kibr) - I_c/I_\mathrm{bias}}{2}
  \label{eq:ch4_stability_condition}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
See Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, Proof~\ref{proof:87l_stability}.
\end{proof}

The proof shows that for $\kibr \leq 0.5$, the stability condition
\eqref{eq:ch4_stability_condition} is always satisfied for
$\epsilon_\mathrm{CT} \leq 5\%$ (IEC Class 5P CT limit). For
$\kibr > 0.5$, the condition requires $\epsilon_\mathrm{CT} \leq 2.5\%$,
which corresponds to Class 1P CTs. Deploying Class 1P CTs on lines with
$\kibr > 0.5$ is therefore a design requirement of the SAMBP system.

\section{Coordination with Transformer Differential (87T)}
\label{sec:ch4_87t_coord}

When the protected line terminates at a transformer, the 87L and 87T zones
may overlap. The 87L zone typically covers the line plus the transformer
leakage reactance (extended reach). Coordination requires:
\begin{itemize}
\item 87T operates for transformer internal faults.
\item 87L resets within 50~ms when the fault is cleared by 87T
  (loss of differential current after transformer trip).
\end{itemize}
The GOOSE status message from the transformer IED is used to coordinate
this reset, avoiding breaker failure initiation.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:ch4_summary}

This chapter has derived the physics of IBR-adaptive line differential
protection:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Adaptive slope} $k_m^\ast(\kibr)$ derived from the
      simultaneous stability and sensitivity constraints, eliminating
      the need for conservative fixed settings.
\item \textbf{87LH} harmonic discrimination resolves the IBR fault vs.\
      transformer inrush ambiguity.
\item \textbf{87LN} negative-sequence differential benefits from IBR
      negative-sequence suppression: Proposition~\ref{prop:ch4_87ln}
      proves $\rho_2 = 2$ for IBR-suppressed asymmetric faults.
\item \textbf{TDCS-87L} transient differential signature covers three-phase
      faults on IBR-only lines where sequence elements provide no coverage.
\item Stability is guaranteed for Class~1P CTs (Proposition~\ref{prop:ch4_stability}).
\end{enumerate}
