\chapter{Inverter-Based Resource Modelling for Protection Analysis}
\label{ch:modelling}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:ch2_intro}

An accurate mathematical model of the IBR is the prerequisite for designing
protection algorithms that respect the physical constraints imposed by power
electronic interfaces. This chapter develops the models used throughout the
thesis, organised from the inner current loop (microseconds) to the outer
grid interaction (milliseconds to cycles). Three levels of abstraction are
introduced:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \emph{switching-average} model used in time-domain simulation,
\item the \emph{sequence-network} phasor model used in relay algorithm derivation, and
\item the \emph{reduced closed-loop} model used in digital-twin state estimation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Synchronous Generator Reference Model}
\label{sec:ch2_sg_model}

For comparison, the synchronous generator (SG) sub-transient fault model
provides the performance baseline. The Thevenin equivalent at the generator
terminals for a three-phase fault at time $t=0^+$ is:

\begin{equation}
  I_\mathrm{sc}^\mathrm{SG}(t) = \frac{E_0}{Z''_d}\,e^{-t/T''_d}
  + \left(\frac{1}{Z'_d} - \frac{1}{Z''_d}\right)E_0\,e^{-t/T'_d}
  + \left(\frac{1}{Z_d} - \frac{1}{Z'_d}\right)E_0
  \label{eq:ch2_sg_fault}
\end{equation}
where $Z''_d$, $Z'_d$, $Z_d$ are sub-transient, transient, and synchronous
direct-axis reactances; $T''_d$ and $T'_d$ are the corresponding time
constants; and $E_0$ is the pre-fault internal EMF. For a typical large SG,
the initial peak fault current is 6--10~pu and decays over hundreds of
milliseconds. This large, passive, decaying fault current is the canonical
input for which legacy relay algorithms were calibrated.

\subsection{Sequence Network of the Synchronous Generator}

Under unbalanced faults, the SG is characterised by three Thevenin
sequence impedances $Z_1^\mathrm{SG}$, $Z_2^\mathrm{SG}$, $Z_0^\mathrm{SG}$
with $Z_1 \neq Z_2 \neq Z_0$ in general. The positive-sequence voltage
source $E_1 = E_0$ drives the sequence network; the negative- and
zero-sequence networks are passive. The grounding impedance $Z_n$ appears
in the zero-sequence network as $3Z_n$.

\section{Grid-Following Inverter Model}
\label{sec:ch2_gfl}

A Grid-Following (GFL) inverter is a current-controlled voltage-source
converter that synchronises to the grid voltage through a Phase-Locked Loop
(PLL) and injects a regulated current in the stationary $abc$ frame or the
rotating $dq$ frame. Figure~\ref{fig:ch2_gfl_structure} shows the block
diagram.

           inner current loop (PI+decoupling) generates the modulation
           reference $v^\ast_{dq}$. The PLL locks to the PCC voltage.}
  \label{fig:ch2_gfl_structure}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Inner Current Loop Dynamics}

In the rotating $dq$ reference frame (angle $\theta_\mathrm{PLL}$ from the PLL),
the converter filter inductor equations are:
\begin{align}
  L_f \frac{d\id}{dt} &= -R_f \id + \omega_0 L_f \iq + v_d - e_d
  \label{eq:ch2_gfl_id} \\
  L_f \frac{d\iq}{dt} &= -R_f \iq - \omega_0 L_f \id + v_q - e_q
  \label{eq:ch2_gfl_iq}
\end{align}
where $L_f$, $R_f$ are the filter inductance and resistance; $v_{dq}$ is the
converter output voltage in $dq$ coordinates; $e_{dq}$ is the PCC voltage;
and $\omega_0 = 2\pi f_0$ is the nominal angular frequency.

The PI current controller with feedforward decoupling is:
\begin{align}
  v_d^\ast &= K_p(i_d^\ast - \id) + K_i \int(i_d^\ast - \id)\,dt
             + e_d - \omega_0 L_f \iq
  \label{eq:ch2_vd_ref} \\
  v_q^\ast &= K_p(i_q^\ast - \iq) + K_i \int(i_q^\ast - \iq)\,dt
             + e_q + \omega_0 L_f \id
  \label{eq:ch2_vq_ref}
\end{align}
The closed-loop transfer function for the current loop is:
\begin{equation}
  G_\mathrm{cl}(s) = \frac{K_p s + K_i}{L_f s^2 + (R_f + K_p)s + K_i}
  \label{eq:ch2_gfl_tf}
\end{equation}
Setting the closed-loop bandwidth $\omega_\mathrm{bw}$ and damping ratio
$\zeta$ gives the controller gains:
\begin{equation}
  K_p = 2\zeta\omega_\mathrm{bw} L_f - R_f, \qquad
  K_i = \omega_\mathrm{bw}^2 L_f
  \label{eq:ch2_gains}
\end{equation}
Typical values: $\omega_\mathrm{bw} = 2\pi \times 500$~rad/s (bandwidth
$\approx$ 500~Hz), $\zeta = 0.7$, giving a current step response settling
time of $\approx 2$~ms.

\subsection{Current Limiter}

The protection of the IGBT switches requires limiting $|i_{dq}|$ to the
rated current $I_\mathrm{rated}$. The priority-based limiter is:
\begin{align}
  i_d^\ast &\leftarrow \mathrm{clip}\!\left(i_d^\ast,\,
             -\sqrt{I_\mathrm{lim}^2 - (i_q^\ast)^2},\,
             +\sqrt{I_\mathrm{lim}^2 - (i_q^\ast)^2}\right)
  \label{eq:ch2_ilim}
\end{align}
where $I_\mathrm{lim} \in [1.1, 1.5]\,I_\mathrm{rated}$ is the hardware
thermal limit. Reactive current $i_q^\ast$ is given priority under
IEEE~1547-2018 to support voltage recovery during faults. This limiter
activates within 2--5~ms of fault inception, after which the fault current
magnitude is constant at $I_\mathrm{lim}$.

\subsection{Phase-Locked Loop}

The synchronous-reference-frame PLL (SRF-PLL) tracks the PCC voltage
angle $\theta$:
\begin{equation}
  \dot{\theta}_\mathrm{PLL} = \omega_0 + K_{p,\mathrm{pll}}\,e_q
    + K_{i,\mathrm{pll}}\int e_q\,dt
  \label{eq:ch2_pll}
\end{equation}
where $e_q = |e|\sin(\theta_e - \theta_\mathrm{PLL}) \approx
|e|(\theta_e - \theta_\mathrm{PLL})$ for small angle error. The PLL
bandwidth is typically $\omega_\mathrm{bw,pll} = 2\pi \times 20$~Hz
(50~ms settling), which is much slower than the current loop. During a
fault, the voltage dip drives a transient PLL error that temporarily
shifts the injected current phase angle by up to 30--45°, further
complicating sequence-based relay algorithms.

\subsection{GFL Fault Current: Phasor Model}

For protection analysis at fundamental frequency (one-cycle window,
20~ms post-fault), the GFL fault current is modelled as a controlled
current source:
\begin{equation}
  \phasor{I}_\mathrm{GFL} = I_\mathrm{lim} \angle(\theta_\mathrm{PLL} + \phi_\mathrm{cmd})
  \label{eq:ch2_gfl_phasor}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_\mathrm{cmd}$ is the commanded power factor angle (0° for unity PF,
90° leading for full reactive support). The key protection-relevant parameter is:
\begin{equation}
  \kibr \triangleq \frac{|\phasor{I}_\mathrm{GFL}|}{I_\mathrm{rated}}
  \in [0, I_\mathrm{lim}/I_\mathrm{rated}]
  \label{eq:ch2_kibr_def}
\end{equation}
which ranges from 0 (converter off) to $I_\mathrm{lim}/I_\mathrm{rated}$
(maximum fault contribution). Under IEEE~2800 mandatory ride-through,
$\kibr$ is always positive during a fault, but its exact value depends on
firmware settings.

\subsection{Sequence Characteristics of GFL under Unbalanced Faults}

Under a single-line-to-ground (SLG) fault, the GFL responds to the
unbalanced terminal voltage $e_{abc}$ through the PLL, which tracks the
positive-sequence voltage. The negative-sequence component of the terminal
voltage does not appear in $\theta_\mathrm{PLL}$ because the PLL uses
$e_q = \mathbf{T}_{dq}(\theta_\mathrm{PLL}) \cdot e_{abc}$ and the
negative-sequence rotates at $-2\omega_0$ in the $dq$ frame. Consequently:

\begin{equation}
  \phasor{I}_2^\mathrm{GFL} = \begin{cases}
    0 & \text{(negative-sequence suppressed by control)} \\
    I_\mathrm{lim}^{(2)}\angle\phi_2 & \text{(IEEE 2800 negative-sequence injection enabled)}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:ch2_gfl_negseq}
\end{equation}
Similarly, the zero-sequence current $\phasor{I}_0^\mathrm{GFL} = 0$ unless
a grounding transformer is present, because the converter output is a
three-wire system. This has critical implications for the 87LN negative-sequence
differential element (Section~\ref{sec:ch4_87ln}) and ground distance
relay (Chapter~\ref{ch:transformer_diff}).

\section{Grid-Forming Inverter Model}
\label{sec:ch2_gfm}

A Grid-Forming (GFM) inverter is a voltage-controlled current source that
generates its own frequency reference, rather than locking to a grid voltage.
The primary GFM architectures relevant to this thesis are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Droop Control:} $\omega = \omega_0 - m_p(P - P^\ast)$,
      $|V| = |V_0| - m_q(Q - Q^\ast)$.
\item \textbf{Virtual Synchronous Generator (VSG)/Machine (VSM):}
      Emulates the swing equation of an SG.
\item \textbf{Matching Control:} DC-bus dynamics matched to rotor angle dynamics.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Virtual Synchronous Machine Model}

The VSM GFM emulates the second-order swing equation:
\begin{equation}
  M\ddot{\theta} + D\dot{\theta} = P^\ast - P_e, \qquad
  P_e = \frac{3}{2}(v_d i_d + v_q i_q)
  \label{eq:ch2_vsm_swing}
\end{equation}
where $M = 2H/\omega_0$ is the virtual inertia constant and $D$ is virtual
damping. The virtual impedance $Z_v = R_v + \jj\omega_0 L_v$ is added in
series to emulate the sub-transient reactance:
\begin{equation}
  v^\ast_{dq} = e_{dq} + Z_v i_{dq}
  \label{eq:ch2_vsm_vi}
\end{equation}
During a fault, the VSM behaves analogously to an SG with sub-transient
reactance $X''_d \approx \omega_0 L_v$. The fault current magnitude is:
\begin{equation}
  I_\mathrm{sc}^\mathrm{GFM} = \frac{|V_0|}{|Z_v|} \approx \frac{|V_0|}{\omega_0 L_v}
  \label{eq:ch2_gfm_isc}
\end{equation}
Because $L_v$ is a software parameter, the GFM operator can choose $L_v$
to obtain any desired $I_\mathrm{sc}^\mathrm{GFM}$. Typical values give
$I_\mathrm{sc}^\mathrm{GFM} \in [1.5, 2.5]\,I_\mathrm{rated}$,
substantially higher than the GFL limit of $[1.1, 1.5]\,I_\mathrm{rated}$.

\section{Sixth-Order Park \texorpdfstring{$dq$}{dq} Truth Model}
\label{sec:ch2_park6}

For high-fidelity digital twin validation (Chapter~\ref{ch:digital_twin}),
a 6th-order closed-loop Park $dq$ model is employed. The state vector is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x} = [i_d,\; i_q,\; \xi_d,\; \xi_q,\; \theta_\mathrm{PLL},\; \xi_\mathrm{PLL}]^\top
  \label{eq:ch2_park6_state}
\end{equation}
where $\xi_{d,q}$ are the current controller integral states and
$\xi_\mathrm{PLL}$ is the PLL integrator state. The state equations are:

\begin{align}
  \dot{i}_d &= \frac{1}{L_f}\bigl[-R_f i_d + \omega_\mathrm{PLL} L_f i_q
    + K_p(i_d^\ast - i_d) + K_i \xi_d + e_d - \omega_\mathrm{PLL} L_f i_q\bigr]
  \nonumber \\
  &= \frac{1}{L_f}\bigl[(K_p - R_f)(i_d^\ast - i_d) + K_i \xi_d\bigr]
  \label{eq:ch2_park6_id} \\
  \dot{i}_q &= \frac{1}{L_f}\bigl[(K_p - R_f)(i_q^\ast - i_q) + K_i \xi_q\bigr]
  \label{eq:ch2_park6_iq} \\
  \dot{\xi}_d &= i_d^\ast - i_d, \qquad
  \dot{\xi}_q = i_q^\ast - i_q
  \label{eq:ch2_park6_xi} \\
  \dot{\theta}_\mathrm{PLL} &= \omega_0 + K_{p,\mathrm{pll}} e_q
    + K_{i,\mathrm{pll}} \xi_\mathrm{PLL}
  \label{eq:ch2_park6_theta} \\
  \dot{\xi}_\mathrm{PLL} &= e_q = |e_{abc}|\sin(\theta_e - \theta_\mathrm{PLL})
  \label{eq:ch2_park6_xipll}
\end{align}

This model has been validated against full switching-model simulations in
\citep{SAMBP_TR45}, showing voltage and current waveform agreement within
0.5\% RMS for $t \in [0, 150]$~ms post-fault. The reduced model deviates
for $t > 150$~ms when AVR dynamics in GFM inverters become significant.

\section{Sequence Network Model for Protection Analysis}
\label{sec:ch2_sequence}

For the derivation of relay algorithms (Chapters~\ref{ch:line_diff}--\ref{ch:digital_twin}),
the IBR is modelled as a sequence-domain controlled current source.
The positive-, negative-, and zero-sequence components of the injected
current are:
\begin{align}
  \phasor{I}_1^\mathrm{IBR} &= I_1 \angle \phi_1
    \quad\text{(controlled; limited to $I_\mathrm{lim}$)}
  \label{eq:ch2_seq_pos} \\
  \phasor{I}_2^\mathrm{IBR} &= k_2 \phasor{I}_1^\mathrm{IBR}
    \quad k_2 \in \{0, 0.5\}
  \label{eq:ch2_seq_neg} \\
  \phasor{I}_0^\mathrm{IBR} &= 0
    \quad\text{(three-wire converter)}
  \label{eq:ch2_seq_zer}
\end{align}
where $k_2 = 0$ when negative-sequence suppression is enabled (IEEE~1547
default) and $k_2 = 0.5$ when negative-sequence support is enabled per
IEEE~2800. The controlled-source model allows closed-form derivation of
relay operating quantities, which would not be possible with the full
6th-order nonlinear model.

\section{Network and Fault Model}
\label{sec:ch2_network}

\subsection{Line Model}

The protected line is modelled as a lumped $\pi$-section with positive- and
zero-sequence parameters:
\begin{equation}
  Z_L = R_L + \jj\omega_0 L_L, \qquad
  Z_{L,0} = R_{L,0} + \jj\omega_0 L_{L,0}
  \label{eq:ch2_line_z}
\end{equation}
with $R_{L,0} = 3R_L$ and $L_{L,0} \approx 3L_L$ for typical distribution cables.

\subsection{Fault Model}

A fault at per-unit location $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ from the relay end (end~A)
with resistance $R_F$ is modelled as a shunt impedance $Z_F = R_F$ at the
fault point. The boundary conditions for a three-phase (3P) fault are:
\begin{equation}
  V_F = R_F\, I_F, \qquad V_F = V_A - \alpha Z_L I_A
  \label{eq:ch2_3p_fault}
\end{equation}
For a single-line-to-ground (SLG) fault on phase~A:
\begin{equation}
  I_{F,a} = I_{F,0} + I_{F,1} + I_{F,2}, \quad
  I_{F,b} = I_{F,c} = 0, \quad
  V_{F,a} = R_F I_{F,a}
  \label{eq:ch2_slg_fault}
\end{equation}
and the sequence currents are equal: $I_{F,0} = I_{F,1} = I_{F,2}$.

\section{IBR Current Ratio \texorpdfstring{$\kibr$}{k\_ibr}: Definition and Operating Range}
\label{sec:ch2_kibr}

The scalar $\kibr$ is the single most important parameter linking IBR
physics to relay algorithm behaviour throughout this thesis. It is defined as:

\begin{thesisdef}{IBR Current Ratio $\kibr$}
The IBR current ratio $\kibr$ is the ratio of the per-unit fault current
injected by all IBRs on one side of a protected zone to the rated current
of that zone:
\begin{equation}
  \kibr = \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}} I_i^\mathrm{IBR}}{I_\mathrm{rated}}
  \label{eq:ch2_kibr_formal}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}$ is the set of IBR units contributing to the fault current
at the relay terminal of interest.
\end{thesisdef}

Based on the current limiter analysis in Section~\ref{sec:ch2_gfl},
the operating range of $\kibr$ for a typical fleet is:

\begin{equation}
  \kibr \in [0.03,\; 0.55] \quad \text{(GFL fleet, operational)}
  \label{eq:ch2_kibr_range}
\end{equation}
The lower bound (0.03) represents a single inverter at minimum curtailed
output; the upper bound (0.55) represents all inverters at their 1.1~pu
limit with a nominal 0.5~pu installed capacity fraction.
For GFM inverters, the range extends to $[0.15, 0.85]$ due to the higher
virtual impedance fault current.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:ch2_summary}

This chapter has established the mathematical models that underpin the
thesis. Key modelling decisions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The GFL inner current loop is modelled as a first-order controlled
      current source with 2--5~ms response time and hard magnitude limit
      $I_\mathrm{lim} \in [1.1, 1.5]$~pu.
\item The GFM is modelled as a voltage source behind virtual impedance
      $Z_v = R_v + \jj\omega_0 L_v$, giving fault currents of 1.5--2.5~pu.
\item For relay algorithm derivation, sequence-domain controlled-source
      models are used with $\phasor{I}_2 \in \{0, 0.5\phasor{I}_1\}$ and
      $\phasor{I}_0 = 0$ for three-wire converters.
\item The 6th-order Park $dq$ model provides a high-fidelity reference
      for digital-twin validation up to 150~ms post-fault.
\item The scalar $\kibr \in [0.03, 0.85]$ (fleet-dependent) is the key
      parameter linking IBR operating state to relay algorithm behaviour.
\end{enumerate}
