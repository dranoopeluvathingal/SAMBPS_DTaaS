The Problem

Conventional Protection Fails in IBR-Rich Grids

Inverter-based resources — DFIG wind, PV, GFM batteries — inject fault currents that are magnitude-limited, phase-shifted, and rapidly time-varying. Three classical assumptions break simultaneously:

The SAMBP Answer

🧮

Inverse Estimation

Two-pass Levenberg-Marquardt fits a physics-based reduced zone model to live fault measurements — no pre-stored lookup tables.

🛡️

Confidence Gate

Jacobian condition number κ_n and residual norm jointly govern whether estimated parameters update relay settings.

🔄

Adaptive Coordination

OC pickup and TMS re-derived per event; graded N-relay coordination enforced by bounded cascade logic.

🏗️

Stage-2 Veto Gate

When the model identifies CT saturation or channel skew rather than a fault, it suppresses the conventional trip — not just delays it.

Methodology

A Uniform Five-Layer Architecture

Every relay family in SAMBP inherits the same design pattern — allowing independent verification at each layer while ensuring the ensemble behaves coherently.

1
Conventional Baseline
Independently operable relay. Always-on. Trip authority intact without any estimation.
2
Reduced Zone Model
Physics-motivated, bounded parameter set. Analytical Jacobian for stability.
87L: θ=[I_fund, φ, I_DC, τ_DC]
3
Two-Pass LM Estimator
Pass 1: full-window global shape. Pass 2: tail window for τ_ac / CT error refinement.
4
Confidence Gate
κ_n < 30 AND residual < ε AND n_confirm ≥ 2 before any parameter update.
Stage-2: model veto if f_int < 0.5
5
Fallback / Coordination
Relay-specific: OC settings for sync, harmonic blocking for 87T, 3-mode FSM for 87L.

Open Research Gaps

Honest Scope Boundaries & Consortium Opportunities

Gap analysis against CIGRE WG B5, IEEE 1547-2018, and Horizon Europe IEC 61850 requirements. Tier 1 gaps represent the highest-value collaboration opportunities.

Tier 1 — Critical

BESS Source Model

Grid-scale BESS (GFM-BESS, VSM mode) produces no natural fault current. All four relay families require new source models. Proposed: TR-68 (BESS 87L + 87B).

Tier 1 — Critical

Type-4 / PMSG Wind

Full back-to-back converter with negligible negative-sequence injection. Inrush blocking, sequence-based protection, and DFIG crowbar logic all need revisiting for Type-4.

Tier 1 — Critical

Meshed / Ring Topology

All current network models are radial. Meshed topology requires directional relaying (67), pilot schemes, and loop-flow aware coordination — structurally absent from the framework.

Tier 1 — Critical

Auto-Reclose (79) on IBR Feeders

Standard 79 logic assumes synchronous recovery. IBR ride-through, delayed reclose, and live-line supervision logic are required for IEEE 1547-2018 compliance. No relay-79 element exists yet.

Tier 2 — Significant

Evolving & Cross-Country Faults

Static fault types only. SLG → 3-phase escalation and simultaneous independent faults are not modelled. IBR current-limiting complicates detection of escalating faults.

Tier 2 — Significant

IEEE 1547 Ride-Through Window

During mandatory ride-through, IBR operates in a controlled low-current regime. Protection must not mal-operate in this window. No structured ride-through compliance study exists.

Tier 2 — Significant

SSCI / Subsynchronous Resonance

SSCI between DFIG wind and series-compensated lines (ERCOT 2009 event). Protection must distinguish resonant oscillation from fault. Frequency range 5–40 Hz overlaps with 64G sub-harmonic work.

Tier 2 — Significant

COMTRADE / Field Data Validation

All validation is against synthetic or ANDES-simulated waveforms. A COMTRADE import pipeline and at least one real disturbance recording are needed to close the lab-to-field gap.

Tier 3 — Emerging

DC Microgrids & Hybrid AC/DC

DC bus protection — di/dt discrimination, DC CB coordination — is a distinct discipline. Relevant for data-centre, EV, and marine microgrids. Entirely absent from current scope.

Tier 3 — Emerging

Cyber-Physical Attacks on GOOSE

GOOSE modelled as ideal. GPS spoofing, replay attacks, and latency injection on the adaptive confidence gate are not tested. IEC 62351 security compliance is increasingly mandated.

Tier 3 — Emerging

GMD / Geomagnetic Disturbance

Quasi-DC GIC causes transformer half-cycle saturation, generating harmonics that can mal-operate 87T inrush blocking. NERC TPL-007 compliance study absent. CIGRE WG C4.204 active.

Tier 3 — Emerging

N-2 / Degraded Relay Set

All studies assume full relay complement. Performance under single-relay failure, CT bypass, or communication loss beyond the 87L FSM fallback has not been quantified.

Consortium & Collaboration

What SAMBP Digital Twin Lab Brings to a Partnership

Developed at the Smart Grid Computational Research Lab (SGCRL), IIT Madras, the SAMBP framework is structured for handoff — each TR is a self-contained study unit with reproducible simulations, explicit identifiability proofs, and quantified uncertainty.

📐

Research-Grade Rigour

Every relay model has an analytical identifiability proof (e.g. Proposition 3.1 for 87B). Parameter count is justified from rank analysis — not heuristic. Reproducible to the seed.

⚙️

Deployment-Ready Architecture

The Stage-2 veto gate and two-pass LM pipeline are designed for real-time relay execution. Signal chain runs in < 1 power cycle on standard hardware. IEC 61850 GOOSE integration included.

🔭

Transparent Roadmap

Gaps are documented publicly. BESS, Type-4 wind, meshed topology, and field-data validation are scoped as TR-68+. Consortia can adopt, fund, or co-develop specific TRs.
